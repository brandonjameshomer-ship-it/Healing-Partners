# Design constraints

What can actually be made. **Check this before proposing any element.** Letting a family fall in
love with something that cannot be cut, or that the cemetery will refuse, is worse than offering
them nothing.

---

## Technique by stone

| Technique | Works on | Never propose on |
|---|---|---|
| **Sandblasting (v-cut, frosted)** | Any granite colour or finish | — |
| **Photo-realistic laser etching** | Near-uniform **absolute black** granite only | Light, grey, patterned, or speckled stone |
| **Hand-drawn / line-art etching** | Most colours; strongest on dark stone | Heavily patterned stone |
| **Porcelain portrait (inset)** | Any colour — it is a mounted physical tile | — |

**The laser-etching rule is the one most often broken.** A photo-realistic portrait relies on
contrast between the etched white surface and the black stone beneath. On grey, blue, or patterned
granite the image loses its ground and turns to mud.

So: if the family wants a photographic likeness, either steer to absolute black, or offer a
porcelain portrait, which works on any stone. Say which is which and why — do not silently
substitute one for the other.

## Marker types

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Upright monument** | Die (the standing tablet) plus base. Priced separately; both need install. |
| **Slant marker** | Angled face, lower profile. |
| **Bevel / pillow** | Low, gently sloped. Often the cemetery's minimum. |
| **Flat / grass marker** | Flush with ground. Common where uprights are prohibited. |
| **Companion** | Any of the above, sized for two. |

**Companion memorials run roughly 5–8 feet wide** and need **two inscription panels planned side
by side** from the start. Do not design a single-person layout and widen it — the composition is
different, and a shared surname banner across the top usually anchors it.

## Second interments

When a monument already exists — typically a surviving spouse.

- The new work must **match the existing stone**: same granite, same lettering style, same cut
  depth. Varying granite colour here is an error, not a design choice.
- A **rubbing or high-resolution photograph of the existing lettering is required before cutting.**
  Make this a blocking line on the order form.
- Vary the emblem composition instead if you need to offer genuine alternatives.
- Confirm whether the stone can be inscribed **in place** or must be removed. This changes both
  cost and timeline.

## Accuracy — the errors that matter

These get carved permanently and cost a full replacement.

- **Never imply military service that did not happen.** A WWII history enthusiast is not a veteran.
  A service emblem on a civilian's stone is a serious, offensive error. If someone mentions war
  history, ask directly whether they served.
- **Never assume a faith tradition** from a name, ethnicity, or a single anecdote. Ask.
- **Never assume family relationships.** Confirm before engraving "Beloved Husband."
- **Confirm name spelling and dates in writing**, then read them back again before ordering.

## Cemetery restrictions

Rules vary by cemetery and often by **section within a cemetery**. Common ones:

- Flat markers only in certain sections
- Maximum height, width, or thickness
- Minimum base dimensions
- Approved materials, sometimes approved colours
- Restrictions on vases, benches, and glass
- Single versus companion footprint limits

**Establish the cemetery and section in step 1** and confirm the rules before finalising. A design
the cemetery refuses is a total loss.

Note who is responsible for checking — the funeral director, the monument dealer, or the family.
Do not assume it has been done.

---

## Lead time interactions

- Bronze runs **parallel** to granite (~10 weeks), so order simultaneously.
- Overseas granite (7–10 months) may not meet an anniversary or unveiling date. **Raise this
  early**, before they are attached to the stone.
- Ground conditions and frost can delay setting regardless of production.
- Second-interment lettering is faster than new monuments, but only after the rubbing is done.
