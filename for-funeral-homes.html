How it works

It asks about the person, not the product.

A thick catalogue isn't a service — it's the reason families can't decide. So the software does the narrowing instead of asking the family to do it.

  1. They tell you about them

    "She fed everyone who walked through the door." That's enough to start.

  2. It asks two more things

    The questions a good interviewer would ask. What did their hands look like?

  3. It narrows the options

    One suggestion at a time. Yes, maybe, or no — with the reason attached.

  4. Three memorials come back

    Real granite, real sizes, priced from your supplier's sheet. Then the order form.

Walk through it Use someone you knew. It works better.

The question everyone asks

Whose information is this, and where does it go?

Short answer: it stays yours.

  • Each funeral home sees only its own families. Your neighbour down the road cannot see your orders, your prices, or what you're billed.
  • Your counselors see the orders they're working on. Nothing else.
  • Families see their own memorial and nothing about your business.
  • That separation is enforced by the database itself, not by the screens — so it holds even if someone goes looking.
  • What a family writes about the person is never sold, never used for advertising, and never sent anywhere raw. Identifying details are stripped before anything leaves.
